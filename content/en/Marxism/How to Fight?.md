## Introduction

[[Reform or Revolution?|The struggles to improve people’s living conditions are essential for overcoming capitalism]] and building a more equitable and sustainable social system. The manifestations of inequality and exploitation are highly diverse, both in terms of their nature and their spatial distribution: the expression of power differences between social groups varies radically depending on the geographic area and the nature of the discrimination (for example, sexism in Afghanistan and the U.S. takes different forms, even though it stems from the same root cause). Managing the struggle to end oppression is difficult, and it is necessary to understand the best approach to achieving this goal.

## The Links Between Struggles

At first glance, it might seem that there is no explicit link between sexism, racism, speciesism, ableism, homotransphobia, and capitalism. Although several of these hierarchies have pre-capitalist origins, capital has not passively endured them but has influenced them and been influenced by them in an opportunistic and instrumental way: the creation of an enemy to fight and the construction of rigid, top-down hierarchies are elements that allow for the diversion of class consciousness, including through material benefits: those who uphold social hierarchies are promised a material dividend, even if this is insignificant compared to class differences.
[[The Roots of Inequalites#Capitalism and Patriarchy|Sexism has been integrated into unpaid care work, which has made it possible to reduce the cost of reproducing the workforce.]]
[[The Roots of Inequalites#Capitalism and Racism| On the other hand, racism has allowed Capital to obtain cheap labor without legal protections, even though racial discrimination is not structurally part of the logic of capitalist reproduction.]]
Furthermore, while analyzing the condition of nonhuman animals, it is evident that speciesism has enabled the exploitation of sentient animals by transforming them into constant capital and raw materials at virtually no cost. Although animals do not receive a wage, they are subjected to forced metabolic and physical labor, from which Capital extracts a profit [^3].  Speciesism is the oldest of the systems of oppression, having developed independently on multiple occasions with the discovery of agriculture and animal husbandry and having been reinvented repeatedly with the succession of production systems. In any case, capitalism has brought the exploitation of animals to its peak through the industrialization of production and the massive use of artificial selection.
Ableism has its roots in the productive needs of capitalism. Those whose bodies or minds do not conform to rigid and uniform labor standards are marginalized and subjected to a process of social exclusion [^2]. Furthermore, people with disabilities are relegated to a state of dependence on their families to relieve Capital of the costs required to make public spaces and production accessible.
Finally, homophobia and transphobia are deeply rooted in a system of religious and cultural values that, at first glance, appear to have no connection to economic factors; however, there are economic elements that have fostered a heteronormative and cisnormative culture, such as the nuclear family—used as the basic economic unit—and the rigid gender division of labor [^1].
The trajectory of contemporary capitalism is characterized by an attempt to co-opt these struggles, seeking to achieve social peace and defuse political and social pressures by assimilating emancipatory demands into circuits of consumption and symbolic representation, without undermining the underlying economic structure.

## The Overton Window

The Overton Window—that is, the set of ideas generally accepted and considered reasonable within society—is a very important tool because it describes the degree to which a certain idea is tolerated. If the fundamental goal of political work is to shift this window so that it aligns with one’s own ideologies, then it is necessary to maintain a pragmatic and flexible approach, without losing sight of one’s ideological foundation: although some goals are interdependent and the complete elimination of exploitation requires the dismantling of interconnected systems of oppression, the intermediate processes of change may have different timelines and methods of implementation depending on circumstances and material conditions. In any case, history shows that the best way to shift the Overton window is to act through the so-called anchoring effect, which consists of shifting society’s attention to a certain issue with deliberately extreme ideas, in order to make the more moderate versions of those ideas more acceptable: for example, if the idea of a post-monetary society were to become a topic of public debate, the proposal for a more generous and comprehensive welfare state would be more acceptable. Another effect to consider is “agenda setting” [^5], which involves influencing society’s view of a certain issue by introducing it into public debate so that people discuss it and regard it as a relevant topic. These two processes can quickly make ideas that would otherwise be considered too extreme more acceptable, as happened in the United States with the MAGA movement and Donald Trump.

## Intersectionality and Pragmatism

Since not all struggles proceed at the same pace, it is essential that they do not hinder one another. This does not mean ignoring some struggles in favor of others or treating them as if they did not influence one another, but rather adjusting one’s approach to foster changes that exacerbate the contradictions of capitalist production, without losing sight of the others. When setting priorities, it is also strategic to consider the ability to awaken class consciousness among the masses, to prevent anti-capitalist ideals from being co-opted by capitalism in a watered-down form [[The Roots of Inequalites|that does not incorporate the economic dimension.]]
Depending on who one’s allies are, one must adopt a different stance: on the one hand, one must promote intersectional consciousness and an awareness of the interconnectedness of struggles; on the other hand, one must strive not to alienate political allies when they are essential to achieving the ultimate goal, which is not to maintain one’s purity but to bring about concrete change in favor of oppressed social groups. However, one must not lose one’s radicalism: compromises must never result in a worsening of the conditions of the oppressed or in their political fragmentation. Otherwise, there are two possible scenarios, both negative: the first is a regression toward intolerance, as has been happening in recent years with the “black wave", and the second is co-optation by the capitalist system into forms of formal equality through which true material emancipation is not achieved and conditions of economic exploitation remain unchanged.
